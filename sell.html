The questions worth answering before you list

Should I fix it first?

Sometimes. Often not. The renovations that return more than they cost are a short list, and it’s a different list at every price point. I’d rather talk you out of a $30,000 kitchen than watch you spend it and not get it back.

What’s it going to cost me to sell?

Commission, attorney, transfer tax, and whatever concessions the deal ends up needing. I’ll put real numbers on a page for your house specifically so there are no surprises at closing.

Do I need to sell before I buy?

Depends on your equity, your financing, and how much uncertainty you can live with. This is the single most consequential decision most move-up sellers make and it deserves an hour of conversation, not a rule of thumb.

How long will it take?

That depends on your price point, your town, and your condition. I’ll give you the current numbers for houses like yours rather than a market-wide average that describes nobody.

No obligation

Request a valuation

I’ll come back with a range and the reasoning. If you’re a year out, say so — that’s a useful conversation too, and I won’t chase you.

A valuation is my professional opinion of market value, not an appraisal. It isn’t a guarantee of what your home will sell for, and it isn’t a prediction of future value. Requesting one doesn’t commit you to anything.
