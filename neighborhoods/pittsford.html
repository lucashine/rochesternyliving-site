Pittsford

Pittsford is the oldest of Monroe County’s ten incorporated villages, sitting on the Erie Canal southeast of the city inside a much larger town of the same name. The village grew around the canal, and most of its industry moved to the north bank, to the stretch now called Schoen Place.

What housing is here

The village is historic homes on walkable streets, much of it inside the Pittsford Village Historic District on the National Register. The town around it is mostly larger-lot subdivisions from the postwar decades forward, plus newer construction and a handful of townhouse and condo communities.

What makes it Pittsford

Schoen Place is the old canal port: a narrow street along the north bank lined with the old coal tower, the concrete grain elevator, the flour mill, and the buildings that now hold shops and restaurants. Locals pronounce it “shane,” after the family that ran a lumber, coal, and produce business there in the early 1900s. Port of Pittsford Park sits right across the water.

Property taxes

In New York the tax bill is built from layers: town, county, and school district, plus a village tax if the house is inside a village. Each layer has its own rate, so two houses at the same price can carry very different bills depending on which side of a line they sit on. Before you make an offer, I’ll pull the actual tax bill for that specific house so it never arrives as a surprise.

Getting around

Route 31 (Monroe Avenue) runs through the village and over the canal at Schoen Place, and I-490 crosses the town, so downtown Rochester and the east-side expressways are both close.

Parks, trails, and getting outside

The Erie Canalway Trail is the main event, with access all along Schoen Place. The canal in the village widens into a basin that’s wider than most canal ports, which is why the waterfront feels as open as it does.

Schools

Pittsford is served primarily by the Pittsford Central School District, with some addresses at the edges of town in neighboring districts. Verify the district for any specific address before you rely on it.

The honest trade-off

Schoen Place is everyone’s favorite canal stop, and the village’s own planning documents name parking there on summer weekends as a real headache. Older village homes also come with older-home realities: plaster walls, smaller closets, and systems you’ll want inspected closely.
