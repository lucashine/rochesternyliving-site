Penfield

Penfield sits east of the city, bordered by Webster to the north, Perinton to the south, Walworth to the east, and Irondequoit and Brighton to the west. It was a mill town before it was a suburb: Irondequoit Creek drops about 90 feet through the old center of town, and that waterpower is what brought Daniel Penfield here around 1800.

What housing is here

Penfield never incorporated a village, so there’s no older downtown grid. Closer to the Brighton and Irondequoit lines you’ll find postwar ranches and capes on regular suburban lots. Moving east, lots get bigger and the houses get newer, with colonials, contemporaries, and newer subdivisions and patio-home communities. The creek valleys mean a lot of wooded and rolling lots, which matters if you want a flat backyard.

What makes it Penfield

The historic center is the Four Corners, where Five Mile Line Road crosses Route 441. The Daisy Flour Mill on Blossom Road is the last of more than a dozen mills that once ran on the creek, and it made flour until 1972. It’s on the National Register of Historic Places.

Property taxes

In New York the tax bill is built from layers: town, county, and school district, plus a village tax if the house is inside a village. Each layer has its own rate, so two houses at the same price can carry very different bills depending on which side of a line they sit on. Before you make an offer, I’ll pull the actual tax bill for that specific house so it never arrives as a surprise.

Getting around

Route 441 (Penfield Road) is the main east-west road into the city, and I-590 runs along the western edge of town. Route 250 is the north-south road through the middle.

Parks, trails, and getting outside

Ellison Park, a Monroe County park, has its main entrance on Blossom Road just east of I-590, with trails along Irondequoit Creek. Channing H. Philbrick Park (it used to be called Linear Park) follows the creek gorge right where it drops through the old mill district.

Schools

Penfield is served primarily by the Penfield Central School District, with some addresses at the edges of town in neighboring districts. Verify the district for any specific address before you rely on it.

The honest trade-off

No village means no walkable downtown. Almost every errand is a drive, and Route 441 carries a lot of the town’s traffic at rush hour. If walking to coffee matters to you, look at Fairport or Pittsford first.
